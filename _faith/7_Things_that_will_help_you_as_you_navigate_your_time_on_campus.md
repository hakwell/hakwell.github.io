University life is new for many students. It is a season of adjustment. There are questions about whether the right program was chosen, pressure to make friends, and the constant feeling of trying to figure things out.

But something deeper is happening during these years.

University doesn't just provide academic knowledge; it shapes one's outlook on the world. Whether intentionally or not, a worldview is being formed—ideas about truth, morality, success, purpose, and identity. These concepts will inevitably influence how you live, the decisions you make, what you pursue, and what you ultimately value.

If you're like me, this stage of life also raises deeper questions. What is the purpose of life? Why am I here? What truly matters? Is life simply to be born, go to school, go to university, finish, get a good job, get married to a beautiful lady or handsome man, and then finally die? Or is there something more?

Below are seven reflections to help you make the most of your time on campus. Some of these points were shaped by an article from *The Gospel Coalition Africa* titled [“How to Get the Most Out of Your Time on Campus”](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/africa/article/how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-time-on-campus/) by Kevin Muriithi. I have borrowed and adapted a few insights from it while reflecting on our own context.

## 1. Seek a Relationship with God Through His Son, the Lord Jesus Christ

I alluded earlier to life's biggest question: Why are we here? The Bible does not leave us guessing. It tells us that there is a Creator who made this universe and everything in it. We are not accidents. We are not products of chance. We were created by God, and we were created for Him.

This Creator is not distant or indifferent. He invites us into a personal relationship with Him, and He does so through His Son, the Lord Jesus Christ. Scripture teaches that the grand purpose of our existence is to glorify God. It even makes the point that God is to be glorified in ordinary actions such as eating and drinking.

Yet we must be clear that we can never truly glorify God outside of Christ. No amount of morality, academic excellence, social influence, or religious effort can reconcile us to Him. Apart from Christ, we remain in rebellion. Through Christ, we are reconciled. If life is lived in rejection of God, Scripture warns of judgment. But through Jesus Christ, sinners are forgiven, adopted, and brought near.

Knowing God through Christ is not an optional addition to life; it is life itself. For those who are already in Christ, this season is not a time to drift, grow cold, or compartmentalize faith. The intellectual, social, and moral pressures of campus can slowly pull the heart away from devotion to Christ. Do not forsake Him. Cling to Him. Abide in Him. Walk closely with Him.

You must also remember that as believers we have been commissioned to make the good news of Christ known to the ends of the earth. The Great Commission is not reserved for missionaries; it is given to every follower of Jesus. You must see your campus not merely as a place God has sent you to study and excel academically, but as a place where His kingdom is to be made known.

Learn to view campus through a missional lens. It is one of the most strategic environments imaginable because it gathers people from different towns, tribes, social backgrounds, and even nations into one space. As C. T. Studd wrote:

> “Only one life, it will soon be past, and only what’s done for Christ will last.”

## 2. Do What You Need to Do When You Need to Do It

Procrastination is one of the quiet dangers of university life. Freedom without discipline easily leads to delay, and committing to complete tasks faithfully and on time is not only about academic success but also about character formation.

Excellence matters, so work should not be done merely to finish but to be done well. The habits formed during these years often shape one's future personal and professional life.

## 3. Aim at Education, Not Just a Qualification

In every field or career, well-qualified individuals are those who not only know what to do but also know how to do it well. There is a serious concern in Zambia that the education system is producing what Prof. Chirwa once described as “overqualified illiterates.” The phrase sounds strong, but it exposes a real problem. It is possible to accumulate certificates and still lack competence, critical thinking, and practical skill.

This point, which I have adapted from Kevin Muriithi's article in *The Gospel Coalition Africa*, reminds us that the goal of university should not be merely to collect qualifications. Aim to gain knowledge, develop real skills, and cultivate excellence in your field. That is what will set you apart in a world that is often more concerned with paper credentials than with true understanding and ability.

## 4. Make Good Friends

Friends will either help you succeed or pull you in the opposite direction. The Bible warns about keeping bad company. The people you choose to walk closely with during university will shape your character, discipline, and direction.

There are many instances where friendships formed in first year later became significant influences on personal growth and long-term direction in life. The saying is true: show me your friends and I will show you your future. So choose your friends wisely.

## 5. Do Not Shy Away from Asking Questions

Asking questions is not weakness; it is an opportunity to learn. University is a place for intellectual growth, and growth requires humility. Correction should not be resisted, because correction often comes with knowledge and deeper understanding.

This point, like some others mentioned above, was also adapted from the article by Kevin Muriithi published on *The Gospel Coalition Africa*.

## 6. Keep Your Priorities Straight

It is easy to become a busybody on campus, constantly moving from one thing to another without direction. But I believe that campus life is supposed to teach us a bit about organisation if we approach it intentionally.

One approach that was very helpful for me, which I learnt while on campus, was organising life around roles and goals. By identifying the key roles I had—whether as a Christian, a student, a friend, or a family member—and setting clear goals for what I wanted to achieve in each, I was able to bring structure to my days.

This method helped me manage my time and energy wisely and gave purpose to my activities while on campus.

## 7. Find a Local Church to Be Part Of

Dear Christian, do not neglect the gathering of the saints, as the Bible says, for the Christian life is not meant to be walked alone. The church provides community, teaching, accountability, and encouragement.

University can be spiritually destabilising if one is disconnected from a healthy church. Being part of a local church anchors your faith, strengthens your conviction, and reminds you that you belong to something far bigger than yourself. This is the third point I borrowed and adapted from the article mentioned above.

## Conclusion

University years will pass quickly. The question is not whether they will pass, but how they will be used. These years can be spent drifting, reacting, and merely surviving, or they can be approached thoughtfully, with clarity of purpose, disciplined habits, wise friendships, and a deep commitment to Christ.

These years will shape much of the rest of your life, so it is worth approaching them with intention and care.
